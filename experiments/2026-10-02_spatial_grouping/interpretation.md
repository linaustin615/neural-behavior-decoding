The spatial-patch experiment did not justify changing the learning model. Across 48 matched fits, unrestricted summaries gave mean test MSE 0.36584; spatial patches gave 0.38002; random groups gave 0.38125; and depth-matched random groups gave 0.38201. All four retain neuron IDs and the same true-coordinate embeddings, so this tests the additional effect of grouping.

Correct patches beat each random control in 7 of 12 matched runs, but the mean advantages were only 0.32% and 0.52%, with intervals spanning benefit and harm. Spatial patches beat unrestricted summaries in only 3 of 12 runs and had 3.88% higher error on average. Both frozen scientific gates failed. These results come from one repeatedly examined recording, not independent animals.

The mechanism did work internally. Attention participation rank rose from about 1.00 for unrestricted summaries to 5.75 for spatial grouping. Replacing local summaries with the global mean increased spatial-model test MSE by about 0.835. This demonstrates reliance on the grouped information under that perturbation; it does not show that anatomy is uniquely useful, because random grouping also created distinct summaries and ablation sensitivity.

A separate linear decoder that retained all summary outputs did not rescue the spatial model: its MSE was 0.39983, versus 0.39717 for a refitted decoder of averaged summaries and 0.38002 for the original trained head. The unrestricted model's separate-output probe was slightly better on test (0.36106), but not validation, and its paired improvement interval included zero. That is an exploratory lead, not a reason to select it using the test set.

The artificial regional-interaction task exposed optimization limitations. Some models stayed near chance under early stopping. Repeating only those runs for the full 50 epochs produced learning, while retaining identical earlier training histories. Spatial models then reached R² around 0.73–0.74, below the predeclared 0.75 threshold; random grouping reached roughly 0.97–0.98. This particular architecture is not a strong demonstration of preferentially learning regional interactions.

The first training-derived principal component alone predicted speed poorly (R² 0.18); the first 16 reached R² 0.76. That weakens the narrow explanation that the first population component alone makes spatial detail unnecessary. It does not establish that anatomy supplies the missing information.

Keep the existing strong decoder as the reference. A future spatial study should test a specific biological relationship and obtain another recording before making a general spatial-benefit claim. Activity-derived grouping, learned using training data only, remains a different untested hypothesis here. These results do not warrant adding the tested patch mechanism to the application code.
